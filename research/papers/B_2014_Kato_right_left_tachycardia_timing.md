# Kato et al. 2014: Earlier tachycardia onset in right vs left mesial temporal lobe seizures (OLDER, pre-2018; supplementary)

## Citation & Link
- Kato K, Jin K, Itabashi H, Iwasaki M, Kakisaka Y, Aoki M, Nakasato N. "Earlier tachycardia onset in right than left mesial temporal lobe seizures." *Neurology* 2014 (volume/pages not verified).
- DOI: [10.1212/WNL.0000000000000864](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000000864)
- Open access: not found. **All numbers are from the abstract only** ([Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=(TITLE:%22Earlier%20tachycardia%20onset%22)&format=json&resultType=core)).

## Venue type
Peer-reviewed journal (Neurology). **Older work**, included because it gives signed timings of HR onset relative to EEG onset, which is the "newer study on early ictal HR vs EEG onset" requested alongside Leutmezer 2003.

## Problem
Is the presence and timing of peri-ictal HR change a lateralizing sign in mesial TLE?

## Dataset
- 21 patients (13-67 y) with MRI-lesional mesial TLE (hippocampal sclerosis 20, amygdala hypertrophy 1); 77 focal seizures without secondary generalization (29 right, 48 left) from long-term video-EEG ([abstract](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000000864)).

## Approach
Retrospective measurement of HR-increase onset time and time of max HR, relative to ictal scalp-EEG onset.

## Evaluation protocol
Group comparison right vs left seizures (statistical testing); no detector.

## Key results (numbers) — abstract only
- HR abruptly increased in **29/29 right** and **42/48 left** seizures.
- HR-increase onset relative to EEG onset: **-11.5 ± 14.8 s (right; i.e. before EEG)** vs **+9.2 ± 21.7 s (left; after EEG)**, p < 0.0001.
- Time of maximum HR: 36.0 ± 18.1 s (right) vs 58.0 ± 28.7 s (left).
- Max HR change from baseline: 47.5 ± 19.1 vs 40.8 ± 20.0 bpm (n.s.).
- Source: [abstract](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000000864)

## Limitations
Small, single-centre, mTLE only, no secondary generalization; retrospective.

## Relevance to our project
- The "HR precedes EEG" effect is **not universal**: it is on average early in right mTLE and late in left mTLE. Our early-warning analysis must report the distribution of (HR onset - EEG onset) and not just the mean.
- TUSZ has no lateralization label per se, so this is a source of unexplained variance in our timing estimates.
- The magnitude (~40-50 bpm) matches the >50 bpm "responder" criterion used by Jeppesen et al.
